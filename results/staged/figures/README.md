## Reading the plots

The `*_discharge.png` plots put the date on the horizontal axis and daily discharge in mm/day on the vertical axis. They help us see changes over time, low-flow periods, and sharp peaks that deserve a closer look. Discharge is normalized by catchment area, rather than shown as a volume per second.

The `*_distribution.png` plots group discharge into 50 bins and show how many observed days fall in each range. Their vertical count axis uses a logarithmic scale, which makes rare high-flow values easier to see alongside common low and moderate flows. Compare the axis ranges when looking across rivers; each plot is scaled separately.

Before plotting, the notebook sorts the dates, aligns each basin to a daily calendar, and treats negative discharge as missing. Missing observations appear as gaps in the time series and are excluded from the histograms. They are never replaced with zero.

These are plots of historical observations. They help us understand the data used for forecasting; they do not compare predicted and actual discharge or identify confirmed flood events.

To regenerate all six PNGs, run the visualization notebook from the beginning after creating the processed table. It saves the figures here at 150 dpi and replaces files with the same names.
